A message alone does not grant a license. It starts the conversation.

## 4. How the agreement works

1. You email your intended use and requirements.
2. The rights holder replies about scope, rights, and commercial terms.
3. You agree on the terms and satisfy any agreed payment conditions.
4. You receive an **explicit written license** — an email confirmation or a signed grant (a template is in [`COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md`](COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md)).
5. You receive or get access to the source files and agreed documentation under the granted rights.
6. From then on, the written agreement — not this repository notice — governs what you may do.

## 5. What can be negotiated

These are the topics a written agreement can address. None of them is automatically available or included; actual rights are exactly what the agreement states:

- **Personal or internal evaluation** — a defined trial or internal review period, if expressly granted.
- **Commercial deployment** — permission to run the template live, and where.
- **Deployment scope** — one website, several, or a client-service scope.
- **Modification and customization** — how far you may change the files.
- **Client work and delivery** — whether you may build sites for clients, and whether they receive source.
- **Redistribution or resale** — only if specifically negotiated and granted.
- **Exclusivity** — only if specifically offered and agreed.
